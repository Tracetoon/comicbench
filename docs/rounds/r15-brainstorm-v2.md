# Round 15: brainstorm v2 (PR anchor first, blind ranking judge), assigned and action-shaped layouts, audited bodies

**Date:** 2026-11-26
**Review page:** `results/r15/review/index.html`. Each card's Brainstorm section shows the anchor, each gag's `shows`, the blind judge's reading (✓/✗) and rank, and the assigned layout.

## What changed (against round 14)

- **The PR comes first:**
  - the gags file names the anchor (the change and 1–3 of the PR's own words) before any gag;
  - each gag says where those words are lettered;
  - the check confirms the words are in the PR.
- **The judge is blind:** it sees only the PR and the gags, says which change each shows, and ranks them. The host picks the best-ranked gag whose reading matches the anchor.
- **Assigned layouts:**
  - six new families (columns, tall-left, big-middle, zigzag, shattered, burst);
  - one family assigned at random per comic, never one of the last 3, and weighted away from tiered and staggered.
- **Action panels:** every panel is marked still, move or impact. Move and impact panels need angled, borderless or breakout frames, with a direction cue in the image prompt.
- **Audited body presentations:** 41 non-human archetypes are drawn from their audited presentations, never from species labels.

### What didn't

- **The blind judge recovered the PR from only 133 of 238 gags (56%).** For 11 of the 40 picks, it couldn't recover the PR from any gag, so the best-ranked gag was used with its PR link unrecovered. That is with the anchor words lettered in every gag's `shows`: the situations still read as metaphors.
- **The medium collapsed:** medium index 3.35 (r12: 4.47).
  - sound effects in 53% of comics (r12: 100%);
  - special balloons 43% (r12: 70%);
  - action or motion 28% (r12: 60%).
- **The lead** was the main character in 12 of 40 comics.
- **Arena reasons** (overall; the first comic is r12's):
  - r12 "gives a reviewer both the exact failure and the metadata change in a single joke";
  - r12 "makes the lettering fix legible at a glance";
  - r15 "delivers the stronger genre set piece".

## What we learned

1. **The brainstorm pipeline is the problem, not its judge.** Rounds 14 and 15 both start from a random fantasy situation and fit the PR into it. Both lose to r12 (20% and 18% overall), whose author starts from the PR and builds the scene around it. A blind judge and a lettered anchor didn't close the gap.
2. **Splitting the work across three agents costs craft.** The script author only receives the picked gag. Sound effects, special balloons and motion all fell sharply against r12, where one author designs the joke and the page together.
3. **Layouts and bodies work as designed.** Layout variety went from 6 families to 12, grid pages from 55% to 8%, and bodies were drawn correctly 97% of the time. These are independent of the brainstorm and should be measured on r12's flow.
